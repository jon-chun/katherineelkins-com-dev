Speaking

Talks & keynotes

Katherine Elkins speaks on AI safety, governance, creativity, cultural heritage, democracy, and the future of human-centered AI. She is a NIST AI Safety Institute PI and is available for keynotes, panels, workshops, and expert consultations. Contact: info@katherineelkins.com.

A Track Record

Before the mainstream caught up, Katherine was publishing, speaking, and building.

Sept 2020 Published one of the first systematic experiments on GPT-3 — the month its API became publicly accessible. Asked: can it pass a writer's Turing test?
Now 382 citations. Foundational reference in debates about AI authorship and the "AI ghostwriter effect."
Jan 2021 Created Divabot, a conversational AI art piece, with digital artist Sarah Zucker. Exhibited at the MoMA-affiliated Feral File gallery before the generative-AI art boom.
Early example of artist-AI co-creation now cited in museum and media-art scholarship.
Oct 2022 Invited to the Helix Center to debate whether GPT-class models understand language, alongside Ned Block, Francesca Rossi, and Kyunghyun Cho — one month before ChatGPT launched.
The questions raised at that table became the field's central controversies by early 2023.
Feb 2025 Published one of the first empirical studies showing that agentic AI systems exhibit systematic decision biases influenced by narrative framing and emotional cues.
Accepted at ICML 2025 as agentic-AI safety emerged as a top policy priority.
Featured talk: Katherine delivered an invited lecture at Concordia University, Montreal, on computational humanities and sentiment-arc methodology. The talk is available on Concordia's event page.
